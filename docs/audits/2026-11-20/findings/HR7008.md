## CORE (headline, plainEnglish, howItWorksToday, changes)

### C1. changes row "Buying stock in public companies" / ifPassed — CONDITION DROPPED — MISSTATES
- Summary: "They may not buy those stocks starting 180 days after the bill becomes law. They may still buy broad investment funds, certain trusts and small-business interests."
- Source (CITED Sec. 2, new 13152(a), (c)): "Except as described in subsection (c), no covered individual may purchase a covered investment." ... "(1) Occupational exception.--Notwithstanding subsections (a) and (b), a spouse or dependent child of a Member of Congress may trade any covered investment if such covered investment is-- (A) not owned by a covered individual and if such trade is performed as a function of the primary occupation of the spouse or dependent child; or (B) made as a part of compensation from an employer ... (2) Dividend reinvestments exception.--The requirements of subsection (a) shall not apply to a covered individual with respect to a transaction for the purpose of reinvesting dividends received from a covered investment into the same covered investment automatically or without delay."
- Source (CRS): "This bill generally prohibits ..." and "Certain transactions by covered spouses and dependents are exempt".
- Why: the row states a flat ban and then lists what "they may still buy". The list leaves out the two purchase exceptions the bill itself writes in (dividend reinvestment by any covered individual; occupational/compensation trades by spouses and dependent children). No CORE part mentions either exception. The same flat wording is in the headline ("Bans members of Congress from buying stocks") and plainEnglish ("would bar them ... from buying stock"); those two are short-form and track CRS minus "generally", so I rate them MINOR on their own, but the changes row presents itself as the full account.

### C2. changes row "Voting by mail or another non-in-person method" / ifPassed — CONDITION DROPPED — MISSTATES
- Summary: "The ballot must include a copy of valid photo ID, the last 4 Social Security digits plus a sworn statement, or a notary's ... statement that the voter personally marked it."
- Source (CITED Sec. 3, new 303A(a)(2)(A)(ii)): "submits with the ballot the last four digits of the individual's Social Security number and an affidavit developed and made available to the individual by the State attesting that the individual is unable to obtain a copy of a valid photo identification after making reasonable efforts to obtain such a copy".
- Why: the Social Security option is open only to a voter who swears they could not get a copy of a photo ID after reasonable efforts. The summary gives the content of the notary statement but not of the sworn statement, so the three routes read as a free choice. (Weakest of the three CORE MISSTATES: nothing stated is false, the limiting condition is left off.)

### C3. changes row "When the voting rules start" / ifPassed — NO SOURCE / OTHER — MISSTATES
- Summary: "Military and overseas voters and some voters with disabilities are exempt from the mail-ballot rule."
- Source (CITED Sec. 3, new 303A(a)(2)(B)): "Subparagraph (A) does not apply with respect to a ballot provided by-- (i) an absent uniformed services voter, as defined in section 107(1) of the Uniformed and Overseas Citizens Absentee Voting Act (52 U.S.C. 20310(1)); or (ii) an individual provided the right to vote otherwise than in person under section 3(b)(2)(B)(ii) of the Voting Accessibility for the Elderly and Handicapped Act (52 U.S.C. 20102(b)(2)(B)(ii))."
- Why: the bill names "absent uniformed services voter" only. "Overseas voters" as a separate exempt group appears nowhere in the packet (the word "Overseas" occurs only in the title of the Act cited for the definition). Second half, "some voters with disabilities": the packet does not carry the text of 52 U.S.C. 20102(b)(2)(B)(ii), so who that covers cannot be checked; the Act's title also names the elderly, which the summary leaves out (see C12).

### C9. howItWorksToday item 3 (also headline, plainEnglish, changes rows 1-2) — EXACT WORD CHANGED — MINOR
- Summary: "The bill covers stock in publicly traded companies".
- Source (CITED Sec. 2, 13151(2)(A)): "The term `covered investment' means a security issued by a publicly traded company or any comparable economic interest acquired through synthetic means, such as the use of a derivative, including an option, warrant, or other similar means."
- Why: "security" and the derivative/option/warrant limb are rendered as "stock". CRS and the committee report themselves say "stocks" / "individual stocks", so the plain word has source backing; the derivative limb is dropped everywhere.
