## CORE findings

### C1. howItWorksToday[0] — CONDITION DROPPED — MINOR (borderline; the nearest thing to a misstatement in this summary)

- Summary: "Farmers and ranchers can get federal help to repair farmland damaged by wind, floods, hurricanes, wildfires or
  other natural disasters, when the damage threatens the land or its ability to produce."
- Source (USC 16 §2201(a)): "...to rehabilitate farmlands damaged by wind erosion, floods, hurricanes, wildfires, or other
  natural disasters when, as a result of the foregoing, new conservation problems have been created that (1) if not treated,
  will impair or endanger the land, (2) materially affect the productive capacity of the land, (3) represent damage that is
  unusual in character and, except for wind erosion, is not the type that would recur frequently in the same area, and (4)
  will be so costly to rehabilitate that Federal assistance is or will be required to return the land to productive
  agricultural use."
- Fault: the four tests are joined by "and"; the summary gives tests (1) and (2) joined by "or" and leaves off (3) (unusual,
  not frequently recurring) and (4) (so costly that Federal help is required). The summary's eligibility is wider than the
  statute's. Also "wind" for "wind erosion".
- Why MINOR and not MISSTATES: the sentence is background on a part of the program the law does not change, and its gist
  (damage that endangers the land or its productivity) is right. A stricter reader could score this MISSTATES because of
  the and/or swap.

### C2. changes[4].today ("Wildfires that qualify") — ABOUT THE SOURCES — MINOR

- Summary: "Farmland and forest programs cover wildfires, but the sources do not say clearly how human-caused fires are
  treated."
- Source: USC 16 §2201(a) lists "wildfires"; USC 16 §2206(a)(2) lists "wildfires". Neither addresses cause.
- Fault: wording about the sources shown to the reader. Also "human-caused" is the summary's term; the bill's term is "a
  wildfire that is not caused naturally" (Sec. 2, new subsection (c)(1)).

### C4. howItWorksToday[4] — NO SOURCE / EXACT WORD CHANGED — MINOR

- Summary: "The new law keeps these programs within the Department of Agriculture, which decides eligible costs and whether
  the repair or restoration work qualifies."
- Source (Sec. 2): "...emergency measures to replace or restore farmland or conservation structures requiring an immediate
  response (as determined by the Secretary)"; "75 percent of the cost of the replacement, as determined by the Secretary";
  (Sec. 3) "not more than 75 percent of the cost of the emergency measures, as determined by the Secretary".
- Fault: nothing in the bill "keeps" the programs anywhere; it is silent on where they sit (they are simply the Secretary of
  Agriculture's programs under USC 16 §2201(a)). "Decides eligible costs" is looser than the text: the Secretary determines
  "the cost", not which costs are eligible. "Whether the work qualifies" is a fair reading of "requiring an immediate
  response (as determined by the Secretary)".

### C6. headline, plainEnglish, howItWorksToday[2], [3], changes[2], [3] — CONDITION DROPPED — MINOR

### C7. plainEnglish, second sentence — CONDITION DROPPED — MINOR

## CORE statements checked and found supported

- headline "faster disaster-repair payments": Sec. 2 and Sec. 3 advance-payment options; CRS "additional options to receive
  an advance on cost-sharing payments before carrying out emergency measures".
- plainEnglish "from 25 percent for fencing only to 75 percent for replacement and 50 percent for repair or restoration":
  CRS "75% of the cost of the replacement and 50% of the cost of the repair or restoration. Current law limits advance
  payments to 25% of the cost of the repair or replacement of fencing."
- plainEnglish "private forest owners can now get up to 75 percent upfront": Sec. 3 "not more than 75 percent".
- howItWorksToday[1] (25 percent, fencing only, 60 days): USC 16 §2201(b)(1)–(2).
- howItWorksToday[2] (forest disasters; 75 percent cap): USC 16 §2206(a)(2), (d). "Or similar disasters" stands for "or
  other resource-impacting events, as determined by the Secretary"; treated as fair plain language, not counted.
- howItWorksToday[3] (no advance option before): CRS "Currently, advance payments are not available under the program."
- changes[0].ifPassed, changes[1], changes[2].ifPassed, changes[3].ifPassed: Sec. 2 and Sec. 3 text.
- changes[4].ifPassed: Sec. 2 new subsection (c); Sec. 3(1).
- whyNow: STATUS (Senate passage 24 Mar 2026; House 368–19 on 23 Jun 2026; Public Law 12 Jul 2026); Fischer (FLOOR) "more
  than 800,000 acres and counting have burned", credited to her.
