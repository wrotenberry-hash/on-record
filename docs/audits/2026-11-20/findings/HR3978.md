Packet: `packets/HR3978.txt`. Sources present: CITED Sec. 1, CITED Sec. 2, CONTEXT, HEADINGS, SOURCE CRS, SOURCE FLOOR (Latta, Pallone), SOURCE STATUS.
Sources absent from the packet: REPORT, RULE, CBO, USC. No part is marked MISSING.

**Verdict: MISSTATES** (one EXTENDED finding, a judgment call; everything else is MINOR).

## CORE findings

### C1. changes row 2 ("Plants that isolate plutonium"), `today` — kind 5 ABOUT THE SOURCES — MINOR
- Summary: "The sources do not say they get the shorter path."
- Source: nothing to quote; this is wording about the sources, which the brief lists as a fault in itself.
- Note: it also sits oddly beside row 1 of the same table, which says every domestic reprocessing plant is treated today as a production facility on the longer path. Latta (SOURCE FLOOR) describes the current position for reprocessing facilities generally as "the longer two-step process". So the cell tells the reader the position is unknown when the summary's own other cells state it.

### C2. plainEnglish — kind 1 WRONG CREDIT (one member's description stated as plain fact) — MINOR
- Summary: "Today a future plant that recycles that waste into usable fuel would face a longer two-step federal license path; this bill lets plants that do not isolate plutonium use a shorter one-step path."
- Source (SOURCE FLOOR, Rep. Latta): "clarifying that spent fuel reprocessing facilities would be licensed under the same one-step framework as other fuel cycle facilities rather than the longer two-step process more appropriate for nuclear reactors and facilities that produce special nuclear material for national security purposes."
- Source (SOURCE CRS) says only: "The licensing process under regulations for special nuclear material has fewer steps than the licensing process under regulations for production and utilization facilities."
- The step counts "two-step" and "one-step" come only from Latta's floor statement. CRS supports the direction (fewer steps) but not the numbers. MINOR because the direction is confirmed.

### C3. howItWorksToday item 3 — kind 1 WRONG CREDIT, and cited to the wrong source — MINOR
- Summary: "Future recycling plants now fall under a longer two-step license path for production and use facilities." (cited `CRS`)
- Source (SOURCE CRS): "fewer steps" only; no "two-step". The "longer two-step process" is Latta's wording (SOURCE FLOOR).
- Also: no source states outright what the law is today. It is implied by CRS ("rather than regulations for production and utilization facilities"; "by excluding such facilities from the statutory definition of production facility") and by Latta, who calls the bill "clarifying". No USC text is in the packet to check it against.

### C4. changes row 1 ("License path for certain future recycling plants"), `today` — kind 1 WRONG CREDIT, cited to the wrong source — MINOR
- Summary: "A domestic plant that reprocesses spent nuclear fuel is treated as a production facility, which uses a longer two-step license path." (cited `Sec. 2`)
- Source (CITED Sec. 2): amends the definition by inserting "or reprocessing" and "(B) reprocessing spent nuclear fuel in a manner that does not isolate plutonium". It says nothing on step counts or on "domestic".
- "longer two-step" is Latta only; "domestic" is CRS only ("The bill applies to domestic facilities").

### C5. howItWorksToday item 5 — kind 4 NO SOURCE (explanatory gloss), cited to the wrong source — MINOR
- Summary: "The bill applies only to domestic plants that recycle spent nuclear fuel without isolating plutonium, a material that can raise weapons-spread concerns." (cited `Sec. 2`)
- Source (CITED Sec. 2): "reprocessing spent nuclear fuel in a manner that does not isolate plutonium" — no "domestic", nothing on weapons.
- Source (SOURCE CRS): "The bill applies to domestic facilities that reprocess spent nuclear fuel in a manner that does not isolate plutonium."
- Nearest support for the gloss (SOURCE FLOOR, Rep. Pallone): "the changes here do not apply to any facility that would isolate plutonium nor do they apply to export controls on reprocessing technologies. These important safeguards ensure that our current nonproliferation regime is kept safe and intact."
- No source says in terms that plutonium "can raise weapons-spread concerns"; it is an inference from Pallone's remark, stated as plain fact. "domestic" comes from the CRS summary, which is labelled "Introduced in House" and carries the caveat "based on draft bill text ... may differ from the bill text as introduced"; the passed text in Sec. 2 does not use the word.

### C6. headline — kind 3 CONDITION DROPPED — MINOR
- Summary: "Lets future nuclear-waste recycling plants use a shorter federal license path."
- Source (SOURCE CRS): "license future recycling facilities that reprocess **certain** spent nuclear fuel ... The bill applies to domestic facilities that reprocess spent nuclear fuel **in a manner that does not isolate plutonium**."
- Source (SOURCE FLOOR, Rep. Pallone): "the changes here do not apply to any facility that would isolate plutonium".
- The headline covers all future recycling plants; the bill covers only those that do not isolate plutonium. Rated MINOR because plainEnglish, directly beneath, states the limit. A stricter reader could call this MISSTATES for someone who reads the headline alone.

### Borderline, not counted
- changes row 4, `today`: "Current export controls remain in place under existing law." No source describes current export controls. Pallone says only that the changes do not "apply to export controls on reprocessing technologies". The cell is near-tautological and consistent with that; not counted.
- changes row 3, `ifPassed`: "it does not itself create or fund a facility." No source says this in terms, but the full bill text in the packet (Sec. 1 short title, Sec. 2 definition change) contains no funding or construction provision. Supported by the text; not counted.

## EXTENDED findings

### E1. whoItAffects item 3 — kind 4 NO SOURCE — MISSTATES (judgment call)
- Summary: "Communities near nuclear reactor sites could be affected if future projects recycle spent fuel now stored at those sites." (cited `FLOOR2`)
- Source (SOURCE FLOOR, Rep. Pallone), the only passage near it: "will potentially help us get more energy out of the spent nuclear fuel piled up at reactor sites across the Nation."
- No source mentions communities, neighbours or any local effect. The statement names an affected group and a consequence that no source states, and cites a floor statement that does not say it. It is hedged ("could be"), so a lenient reader might rate it MINOR; I rate it MISSTATES because the reader is told the record identifies an affected group that it does not.

### E2. whatItDoes item 3 — OTHER (wrong name) plus kind 2 EXACT WORD CHANGED — MINOR
- Summary: "Leaves the bill's short title as the Nuclear Recycling Efficient Fuels Utilizing Expedited Licensing Act."
- Source (CITED Sec. 1): "This Act may be cited as the ``Nuclear REFUEL (Recycling Efficient Fuels Utilizing Expedited Licensing) Act''."
- The title is given without "REFUEL" and without the parentheses, so it is not the title in the bill. "Leaves ... as" suggests the section keeps an existing title unchanged; Sec. 1 sets the title ("may be cited as").

### E3. whatChangesForYou — kind 4 NO SOURCE, and kind 1 (Latta's "one-step" as plain fact) — MINOR
- Summary: "A person living near a nuclear plant would not see an immediate change. If a company later proposes a spent-fuel recycling plant that does not isolate plutonium, the federal review would follow a shorter one-step license path."
- No source says anything about people living near a nuclear plant or about the absence of immediate change. It is a reasonable inference from CRS ("no commercial reprocessing facilities currently operating"; "future facilities") but it is not stated.
- "one-step" is Latta's word (SOURCE FLOOR: "the same one-step framework"); CRS says "fewer steps".
