# H.R. 8897 — Improving Travel for American Families Act — audit findings

Packet: `packets/HR8897.txt` (46 lines, 24,558 chars, read in full). Only the packet was used.

**Verdict: MISSTATES** (one CORE statement of current practice has no source; everything else is MINOR).

Counts: CORE 1 MISSTATES, 9 MINOR. EXTENDED 0 MISSTATES, 5 MINOR.

---

## CORE findings

### C1. changes row 3 ("Access to faster screening lanes"), `today` — NO SOURCE — MISSTATES (unsupported, not contradicted)
- Summary: "Passengers may use faster lanes only when their boarding pass shows they are eligible for that faster screening." Also the `ifPassed` cell: "Family-lane passengers **still** may not enter Transportation Security Administration PreCheck or another faster lane unless their boarding pass shows eligibility."
- Source: the only passage on this is a rule the bill would impose **inside the pilot**, CITED Sec. 2(b)(2): "If the Administrator establishes the pilot program under subsection (a), the Administrator shall ... Prevent individuals carrying out screening functions under section 44901 of such title from allowing any passenger into a TSA PreCheck or other expedited security screening lane unless such passenger has received a boarding pass indicating eligibility for TSA PreCheck or such other expedited security screening."
- No source in the packet (REPORT, FLOOR, CRS, USC 49 §44903 as far as it was fetched) states what the rule is today. The "today" cell restates the bill's pilot requirement as existing practice, and "still" in the `ifPassed` cell repeats that. It may well be true, but the packet does not say it.

### C2. headline — EXACT WORD CHANGED / wrong actor — MINOR (borderline)
- Summary: "Lets **airports** test family security lines for travelers with young children."
- Source: CITED Sec. 2(a): "The Administrator of the Transportation Security Administration ... may establish a pilot program **at airports**". REPORT: "would allow the Administrator of the Transportation Security Administration (TSA) to establish a two-year pilot program".
- The permission goes to the TSA Administrator; airports are where the test happens, not who is allowed to run it. The plainEnglish paragraph gets this right ("lets the agency test"), which is why this is rated MINOR rather than MISSTATES.

### C3. plainEnglish — EXACT WORD CHANGED (priority vs consider) — MINOR
- Summary: "giving priority to airports with many travelers with children age 12 or younger **and enough space**."
- Source: CITED Sec. 2(c): "(1) **give priority** to an airport the Administrator determines to have a high volume of passengers traveling with accompanying child passengers; (2) **consider** airports with adequate space to accommodate additional or larger security screening lanes and sufficient personnel ...". REPORT keeps the same split: "prioritize ... airports with high volumes of family travelers and to consider airports with sufficient space".
- Space (and staffing) is something to "consider", not a priority factor. The wording matches Rep. Garbarino's looser floor description (FLOOR: "would be prioritized at airports with high volumes of family travelers, as well as at airports that have the space"), not the bill. The changes row 2 and whatItDoes state it correctly.

### C4. howItWorksToday item 1 — CONDITION DROPPED / subject shifted — MINOR (borderline)
- Summary: "Current law tells it to protect passengers and treat people courteously and efficiently."
- Source: SOURCE USC 49 §44903(b): "The Administrator shall **prescribe regulations** to protect passengers and property on an aircraft ... against an act of criminal violence or aircraft piracy. When prescribing a regulation under this subsection, the Administrator shall— ... (3) **to the maximum extent practicable**, require a uniform procedure for searching and detaining passengers and property to ensure— (A) their safety; and (B) courteous and efficient treatment **by an air carrier, an agent or employee of an air carrier, and Government, State, and local law enforcement personnel** carrying out this section".
- The law tells the Administrator to write regulations; the courtesy/efficiency language is a "to the maximum extent practicable" requirement on the search procedure, about treatment by air carriers and law-enforcement personnel. The summary turns that into a direct duty on the agency to treat people courteously and efficiently, without the qualifier.
- The first sentence of the same item ("The Transportation Security Administration runs passenger screening at airport checkpoints") is not stated in the fetched part of §44903; it is supported only loosely by REPORT ("Under its broad screening authorities, TSA currently offers several alternative lanes to expedite access to security screening services"). Not counted as a separate fault. Note the USC source is cut off in the packet (see Limits).

### C6. changes row 2, `ifPassed` — CONDITION DROPPED — MINOR
- Summary: "The agency **must** give priority to airports with many travelers with children age 12 or younger, and consider airports with enough space and staff."

- The duty only exists if the agency chooses to start the test. Row 1 says "may" and row 5 keeps the "If the agency starts the test" condition, so the meaning is recoverable from context.

## EXTENDED findings

### E1. unknowns item 1 — ABOUT THE SOURCES (and not quite accurate about them) — MINOR
- Summary: "**The sources do not say** how many airports would join the test."
- Source: the House-passed text (CITED Sec. 2) sets no number, so the underlying point holds for the bill as passed. But SOURCE CRS (labelled "Introduced in House") does give a number for the introduced version: "TSA must implement the pilot program at a minimum of five airports". So as a statement about the packet's sources it is not strictly true, as well as being source-talk.

### E2. unknowns item 2 — ABOUT THE SOURCES — MINOR
- Summary: "**The sources do not give** a cost estimate." (True of the packet: there is no SOURCE CBO section and no cost figure in REPORT.)

### E3. whatItDoes item 2 — EXACT WORD CHANGED — MINOR
- Summary: "including **checking each passenger under** the agency's Secure Flight system."
- Source: CITED Sec. 2(b)(1): "Ensure each passenger described in such subsection is **screened in accordance with the vetting status of such passenger under** the Secure Flight program".
- The bill requires screening that matches each passenger's existing Secure Flight vetting status; the summary reads as a Secure Flight check of each passenger. Close, not identical. Also only applies "If the Administrator establishes the pilot program".

### E4. whoItAffects item 2 — NO SOURCE (inferred consequence) + "staff" for "resources" — MINOR
- Summary: "Other airport passengers could see checkpoint lanes adjusted as local managers move staff based on passenger volume."
- Source: CITED Sec. 2(b)(3) only permits local management "to open or close checkpoint lanes and reallocate available resources as needed". No source describes an effect on other passengers of that kind; what REPORT does say about other passengers is that "passengers in both family lanes and regular security screening lanes would see improved wait times and a smoother security experience."

### E5. whatChangesForYou — NO SOURCE (invented example) — MINOR
- Summary: "A parent flying with a **6-year-old and a stroller could be sent to** a family security line at a selected airport."
- Source: no source gives this example. The bill covers "adult passengers and accompanying child passengers twelve years of age and under" and speaks of "expedite access", not of passengers being sent or directed to a line. A 6-year-old is within the age range and strollers are mentioned by Rep. Mackenzie (FLOOR), so the illustration is consistent with the sources. The second sentence ("would still be screened under the same security rules") is supported by Sec. 2(a)/(b) and FLOOR (Mackenzie: "does not change TSA's screening requirements or security standards").

## Limits

- **SOURCE USC 49 §44903 is cut off in the packet.** The header says "3 part(s)" but the text ends mid-word in subsection (c)(2)(A)(ii) ("...the methods the airport operator will use to mon"). Subsection (j) (Secure Flight, which the bill cites) and everything after (c)(2) could not be read. The howItWorksToday item 1 finding (C4) rests on subsections (a)–(b), which are complete. Whether a later part of §44903 states the boarding-pass rule in C1 or that TSA runs checkpoint screening could not be checked.
- No section is marked MISSING. There is no SOURCE CBO or SOURCE RULE section in this packet.
- 49 U.S.C. 44901 and 114 (cited by the bill and the report) are not in the packet.
