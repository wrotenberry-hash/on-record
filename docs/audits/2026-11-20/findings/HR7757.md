# H.R. 7757 (KIDS Act) — claim-by-claim audit against the packet

Packet: `packets/HR7757.txt` (59 lines, 56,159 chars), read in full. Only the packet was used.

## Verdict

**MINOR ONLY** — no statement was found that the packet shows to be false. Two CORE statements rest on bill
sections that are not in the packet (Sec. 201, Sec. 212) and could not be checked; one of them carries an exact age
("under 17") that no packet source states. The whole "today" column and two other CORE sentences are statements about
the sources rather than about the law.

Tally of verifiable faults:

- CORE: 0 MISSTATES, 14 MINOR (8 of them are kind 5, "about the sources")
- EXTENDED: 0 MISSTATES, 8 MINOR (3 of them are kind 5)
- Unverifiable (not counted above): 2 CORE statements, see "Could not check"

## What the packet contains and what it lacks

Present: SUMMARY, CITED Sec. 103, Sec. 214, Sec. 215 (complete), CONTEXT, HEADINGS, SOURCE CRS (of the *Introduced in
House* version), SOURCE FLOOR (truncated), SOURCE STATUS, USC 18 §1591, USC 18 §2256, USC 5 §551 (each cut off in the
amendment notes only).

Absent: Sec. 102 and Sec. 201 (both cited by the summary), Sec. 212 (definitions of minor / child / teen / covered
platform for Sec. 214–215), Sec. 303 (video games), Secs. 402–405 (chatbots), Title V onward (privacy). No SOURCE
REPORT, SOURCE RULE or SOURCE CBO. No section is labelled MISSING, but the floor record breaks off mid-sentence in
Rep. Trahan's remarks ("and they have been left to do") and has "[…]" gaps inside the Guthrie and Pallone remarks; the
CONTEXT lists four further speakers (Lee of Florida, Schrier, Miller of Illinois, Castor of Florida) whose words are
not in the packet. HEADINGS is cut off at Sec. 514.

One point that bears on everything cited to CRS: the CRS summary is of the introduced bill, and the House passed the
bill "as amended" (STATUS). The packet shows the text did change: CRS says "Social media platforms may not allow
ephemeral messaging features for minors", while Sec. 214 in the packet regulates ephemeral messaging (controls, parent
notice, disable for a child) rather than banning it. The summary did not repeat the ban, which is right, but its video
game and chatbot rows rest on CRS alone.

## CORE findings

### C1. plainEnglish — kind 5 ABOUT THE SOURCES — MINOR
- Summary: "Today, the sources do not show one nationwide federal safety rule for minors on social media, online games,
  adult-content sites, or artificial intelligence chatbots."
- Source: none states what current law is on this. Nearest: FLOOR, Rep. Pallone: "expanding these critical privacy
  protections from the current age of 12 to teens up to the age of 17"; FLOOR, Rep. Mcclellan: "The KIDS Act closes
  critical gaps in Federal law".
- Note: literally true of the packet, but it is a statement about the documents. A reader will take it as "no federal
  rule exists today", which no source states (the Pallone line shows some federal privacy protection exists for
  children up to 12).

### C2. howItWorksToday[0] — kind 5 ABOUT THE SOURCES — MINOR
- Summary: "The sources do not list one current federal safety rule covering all of them."
- Source: as C1. (The first sentence, "Children and teens use websites, apps, games, social media, and chatbots run by
  private companies", is background consistent with the floor statements; not counted.)

### C3–C8. changes[0..5].today — kind 5 ABOUT THE SOURCES — MINOR (six rows)
Every "today" cell is a statement about the sources:
- Row 1: "The sources do not show a nationwide federal age-check rule for public sites where more than one-third of

- Row 2: "The sources do not show a nationwide federal rule requiring the most protective default privacy and safety

### C9. plainEnglish — kind 1 WRONG CREDIT (member's description stated as plain fact) — MINOR

### C10. plainEnglish — kind 2 EXACT WORD CHANGED — MINOR
- Summary: "chatbot warnings"
- Source CRS: "providers of AI chatbots must disclose to users who are minors (1) that the chatbot is an AI system and
  not a human, and (2) suicide and crisis intervention hotline information."
- Note: a required disclosure is called a warning. (The changes row for chatbots words it correctly as "tell minor
  users".)

### C11. headline — kind 3 CONDITION DROPPED — MINOR
- Summary: "Requires websites and apps to add safety settings for children and teens."
- Source CRS: "This bill requires specified online platforms to establish safeguards for minors."
- Note: "specified" is dropped, so the headline reads as all websites and apps. The body does narrow it ("covered").

### C12. howItWorksToday[1] — kind 2 EXACT WORD CHANGED / OTHER — MINOR
- Summary: "Current federal criminal law defines a minor as someone under 18 for laws on child sexual images and sex
  trafficking."
- Source USC 18 §2256: "For the purposes of this chapter, the term— (1) "minor" means any person under the age of
  eighteen years". Source USC 18 §1591: "…or that the person has not attained the age of 18 years and will be caused to
  engage in a commercial sex act" — the definitions in §1591(e) (abuse of legal process, coercion, commercial sex act,
  participation in a venture, serious harm, venture) do not define "minor".
- Note: the under-18 definition of "minor" is in §2256 only. §1591 sets an under-18 age line but does not define the
  term. Same age, so same practical meaning.

### C13. changes[1].ifPassed — kind 3 CONDITION DROPPED — MINOR
- Summary: "Covered social media platforms must make the most protective privacy and safety settings the default …"
- Source Sec. 214(a)(3): "the default setting of any safeguard described in paragraph (1) is the option available on
  the covered platform that provides the most protective level of control with respect to privacy and safety".
- Note: the default rule is tied to the six listed safeguards in paragraph (1), not to all privacy and safety settings.
  (The "under 17" in the same sentence is treated separately under "Could not check".)

### C14. changes[2].ifPassed — kind 3 CONDITION DROPPED — MINOR
- Summary: "Platforms must give parents tools to view settings, manage children's settings, restrict purchases, see time
  spent, limit time, and get first-message notices."
- Source Sec. 214(b)(1)–(2): tools are "for a parent of a user of the covered platform who the provider knows is a
  minor"; "(C) The ability to restrict purchases and financial transactions by such user, if applicable"; "(D) … restrict
  time spent on the covered platform by such user, if such time restrictions do not amount to full exclusion of access";
  "(B) In the case of a user that the covered platform knows is a child, manage, change, and control the privacy and
  account settings".
- Note: dropped: "who the provider knows is a minor", "if applicable", and the limit that time restrictions may not
  amount to full exclusion. "Platforms" also drops "covered". The child-only limit on managing settings is kept only
  loosely ("children's settings").

## Could not check (CORE, not counted in the tally)

### U1. changes[1].ifPassed — exact age with no packet source
- Summary: "… the default for users they know are under 17."
- Source Sec. 214(a)(3): "a user of or visitor to the covered platform who the provider knows is a minor". The packet
  does not contain Sec. 212, which defines "minor" for this subtitle. No packet source gives "under 17" for these
  safeguards. The only ages in the packet are: "teens up to the age of 17" and "teenagers under 18" (Pallone and Guthrie,
  about the privacy provisions), "children under 14" (Guthrie), "children under 13" (Miller-Meeks), and under 18 in
  USC 18 §2256. The summary itself uses "under 18" in plainEnglish and howItWorksToday[1].
- If Sec. 212 does not say under 17, this is a wrong number (MISSTATES). It needs a check against Sec. 212.

### U2. howItWorksToday[3] — definition cited to a section not in the packet
- Summary: "The bill's social media section covers public online services where users create searchable accounts, share
  user-made content, and receive ads or recommendations based on personal information." (cited Sec. 201)
- Source: Sec. 201 is not in the packet. Nearest passage is FLOOR, Rep. Miller-meeks, speaking of her SPY Kids Act
  part: "The bill applies to user-generated content platforms that use engagement-driven design features and personal
  data to tailor ads or recommendations, such as social media companies and social gaming platforms." "Public" and
  "searchable accounts" appear in no packet source.

### E5. whoItAffects[1] — kind 3 CONDITION DROPPED — MINOR
- Summary: "Parents of minors on covered platforms would get tools to manage settings, limit purchases, restrict time,
  and receive some message notices." (cited Sec. 214)
- Source Sec. 214(b)(2): "(A) View the privacy and account settings of such user … (B) In the case of a user that the
  covered platform knows is a child, manage, change, and control the privacy and account settings of such user."
- Note: in the cited section, parents of minors may view; managing is limited to a known child. The wording does match
  CRS ("provide tools for parents to manage the privacy and account settings of a minor"), which describes the
  introduced version. Also drops "if applicable" on purchases.

### E6. whatChangesForYou — kind 3 CONDITION DROPPED — MINOR
- Summary: "A parent whose 12-year-old uses a social media app could see and manage account settings, limit time spent,
  restrict purchases, and get notice when another user first tries to message the child."
- Source Sec. 214(b)(1)–(2): applies to a "covered platform" and a user "who the provider knows is a minor" / "knows is
  a child"; purchases "if applicable"; time limits "if such time restrictions do not amount to full exclusion".
- Note: "a social media app" drops "covered", and the knowledge condition is dropped. That a 12-year-old counts as a
  "child" cannot be confirmed from the packet (Sec. 212 absent), though both floor references to "children" use under
  13 or under 14. "Could" softens it.

### E7. whatItDoes[1] — kind 3 CONDITION DROPPED — MINOR
- Summary: "Requires covered social media platforms to give minors safer defaults, message controls, time-limit options,
  and parent tools."
- Source Sec. 214(a)(1): "a user of or visitor to the covered platform who the provider knows is a minor".
- Note: the knowledge condition is dropped here (it is kept in changes[1]). The separate teen messaging controls in
  Sec. 214(c) apply to a user "who the provider knows is a teen"; minors generally get the safeguard in (a)(1)(A).

### E8. caseFor[1] (Rep. Pallone) — kind 3 / looser paraphrase — MINOR
- Summary: "He says platforms keep children connected for profit, and the bill changes incentives by limiting data
  collection and banning targeted ads to minors."
- Source FLOOR, Rep. Pallone: "The KIDS Act also changes the business incentives driving Big Tech's desire to keep kids
  on their platforms by protecting minors' data and expanding these critical privacy protections from the current age of
  12 to teens up to the age of 17. The bill also bans targeted advertising to kids and teens and ensures that websites

- Note: Pallone ties the change in incentives to protecting minors' data and extending privacy protections by age; the
  ad ban and data limits come in the next sentence as "also". The summary folds them into the incentive claim. Correct
  speaker, correct date, same general meaning.
