# Audit findings: H.R. 1013 (Retirement Fairness for Charities and Educational Institutions Act of 2025)

Packet: `packets/HR1013.txt`. Checked against the packet only (CITED Sec. 2, CRS, FLOOR, REPORT, STATUS, USC 15 §77e).

**Verdict: MISSTATES** (CORE 1 MISSTATES, 9 MINOR; EXTENDED 2 MISSTATES, 6 MINOR)

---

## CORE (headline, plainEnglish, howItWorksToday, changes)

### C1. changes row "Review of choices in government plans", ifPassed — EXACT WORD CHANGED — MISSTATES (borderline)
- Summary: "For government plans, the employer, a plan decision-maker, or someone acting for the employer **must** review and approve each investment choice before workers can choose it."
- Source (CITED Sec. 2): the 403(b) plan qualifies for the exemption "(I) if-- ... (cc) such plan is a governmental plan ...; and (II) **if** the employer, a fiduciary of the plan, or another person acting on behalf of the employer reviews and approves each investment alternative offered under such plan described under subclause (I)(cc) prior to the investment being offered to participants in the plan".
- Why: the bill makes review-and-approval a condition a government plan has to meet to come within the new exemption ("if"). It does not lay a free-standing duty ("must") on government 403(b) plans. Row 1 of the same table states it correctly as a condition ("if they meet the bill's conditions, including review of choices in government plans"); this row, read alone, tells the reader the bill creates a new mandatory review for every government plan.

### C2. plainEnglish — WRONG CREDIT — MINOR
- Summary: "**Supporters say** 15 million workers could get lower fees"
- Source (FLOOR, Rep. Wagner only): "a regulatory misalignment continues to lock 15 million nonprofit workers--15 million nonprofit workers--out of a low-cost investment opportunity."
- Why: the 15 million figure is one member's. Other supporters in the packet say only "millions" (Hill) or give no number. The summary credits it correctly elsewhere (whoItAffects: "Rep. Wagner said 15 million ...").

### C3. plainEnglish — WRONG CREDIT — MINOR
- Summary: "**opponents say** plans holding $1.6 trillion would lose standard fee-and-risk disclosures."
- Source (FLOOR, Rep. Lynch only): "These plans hold $1.6 trillion." The other opponent in the packet gives a different figure (REPORT, minority views): "these plans, in aggregate, have over $1 trillion in retirement assets".
- Why: $1.6 trillion is Rep. Lynch's figure alone; the minority's is "over $1 trillion". howItWorksToday and whoItAffects credit the two figures correctly.

### C4. changes row "Review of choices in government plans", today — ABOUT THE SOURCES — MINOR
- Summary: "**The sources do not describe** a specific federal review requirement for every investment option in government-run school and charity retirement plans."
- Source: none; this is a statement about the sources, not about the law.

### C5. changes row "Cost to the federal government", today — ABOUT THE SOURCES (borderline) — MINOR
- Summary: "No cost estimate is available **in the official record**."
- Source (REPORT): "The Committee has requested but not received a cost estimate from the Director of the Congressional Budget Office."

### C6. howItWorksToday item 3 — NO SOURCE / one member's description stated as fact — MINOR
- Summary: "**Both pool retirement money for professional management.**"
- Source (FLOOR, Rep. Lucas): "Collective investment trusts allow investment plans to pool their investments, providing access to institutional investment strategies at lower costs ... Similarly, insurance company separate accounts provide another avenue for retirement plans to access professionally managed investments". (CRS says only that collective investment trusts are "a group of pooled investment assets held by a bank or trust company".)
- Why: pooling is said of the trusts and professional management of the separate accounts; no source says both do both, and the item is credited to CRS, which says neither of separate accounts.

### C7. changes row "Required federal investment registration", today — NO SOURCE — MINOR
- Summary: "Securities, **meaning stocks, bonds and similar investments**, ..."
- Source: no packet source defines "security" (USC 15 §77e uses the word without defining it).

### C8. changes row "Insurer-run investment accounts", ifPassed — CONDITION DROPPED — MINOR
- Summary: "... if the money comes from allowed retirement plan contributions listed in the bill."
- Source (CITED Sec. 2): "separate account the assets of which are derived **solely** from-- ... (iv) contributions to a plan described in clause (iii) or (iv) of subparagraph (D)" — i.e. a 403(b) plan that itself meets the ERISA / employer-fiduciary / governmental-plan-with-review conditions.
- Why: "solely" is dropped and the plan-level conditions are folded into a vague phrase; meaning broadly kept.

### C9. changes row "Review of choices in government plans", ifPassed — EXACT WORD CHANGED — MINOR

## EXTENDED

### E1. whatItDoes item 3 — EXACT WORD CHANGED — MISSTATES (borderline)
- Summary: "**Requires** investment choices in government 403(b) plans to be reviewed and approved before workers can choose them."
- Source (CITED Sec. 2): "(II) **if** the employer, a fiduciary of the plan, or another person acting on behalf of the employer reviews and approves each investment alternative ... prior to the investment being offered to participants in the plan".
- Why: same fault as C1. It is a condition of the exemption, not a requirement the bill imposes on government 403(b) plans.

### E2. whatItDoes item 2 — NO SOURCE — MISSTATES
- Summary: "Lets 403(b) plans use insurance-company investment accounts **that keep plan assets separate from the insurer's general money**."
- Source: nothing in the packet says this. CITED Sec. 2 says only "separate account the assets of which are derived solely from-- ... contributions ..."; CRS says only "insurance company separate accounts".
- Why: a description of how the product works, cited to Sec. 2, which does not state it.

### E3. caseFor item 1 (Rep. Lucas) — CONDITION DROPPED — MINOR
- Summary: "a public servant saving $10,000 a year for 40 years could lose more than $19,000 if fees are 0.05 percentage points higher."
- Source (FLOOR, Rep. Lucas): "**Assuming a 6-percent annual return**, paying just 5 basis points more in **annual** investment fees could cost that worker more than $19,000 in retirement savings."
- Why: the return assumption the figure rests on is left off. (5 basis points given as 0.05 percentage points is a unit conversion the packet does not itself state.)

### E4. caseAgainst item 2 — WRONG CREDIT — MINOR

In addition, they would not be subject to examination and oversight by the SEC and FINRA.''"
- Why: the list is the Consumer Federation of America's analysis, quoted by the minority, not the minority's own words.
