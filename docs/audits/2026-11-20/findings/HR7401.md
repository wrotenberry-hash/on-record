# Audit findings: H.R. 7401, Small Business Lending Fraud Prevention Act

Packet: `packets/HR7401.txt` (read in full; nothing marked MISSING).
Verdict: **MINOR ONLY**. No statement was found that tells the reader something false. Counts: CORE 0 MISSTATES / 7 MINOR; EXTENDED 0 MISSTATES / 10 MINOR.

The one finding closest to MISSTATES is the "certify that they will" versus "must" swap (C1 and E1). It is rated MINOR because the practical meaning is close and Rep. Williams describes the bill the same way on the floor, but it is the one to look at first.

## CORE (headline, plainEnglish, howItWorksToday, changes)

### C1. changes row 2 ("What happens if a conflict appears later"), ifPassed — EXACT WORD CHANGED — MINOR (borderline)
- Summary: "The worker must immediately tell a supervisor and remove themselves from that loan after learning of the conflict."
- Source (CITED Sec. 2(a)(2)): the employee "shall, prior to such participation in such loan, certify to the Administrator in writing that such employee-- ... (2) upon learning of any such conflict of interest after submitting such certification, will immediately disclose such conflict of interest to the supervisor of such employee and recuse themselves from participating in such loan".
- Source (CRS): "to certify in writing ... that the employee ... will disclose any such conflict of interest arising after the certification is made".
- Why: the bill requires a written certification that the worker *will* disclose and recuse; the summary states a direct duty to disclose and recuse. The duty to disclose and recuse is, per CRS, already current law ("Under current law, federal employees must disclose financial conflicts of interest ... and recuse themselves"), so the row presents as a change something the bill only makes the worker promise in writing. Rep. Williams (FLOOR) does describe it as the summary does ("requires SBA employees to certify that they have no conflicts of interest before processing a loan, to disclose any conflicts immediately, and to recuse themselves"), and the summary's own whatChangesForYou gets it right ("must sign that they ... will step away if one appears").

### C2. changes row 4 ("Start dates"), today — ABOUT THE SOURCES — MINOR
- Summary: "No loan-by-loan written certification requirement is in the sources."
- Source: none needed; this is wording about the sources rather than about the law. (The packet supports the substance indirectly: REPORT minority views, "a certification form will provide an extra layer of security".)

### C3. plainEnglish, sentence 1 — EXACT WORD CHANGED + CONDITION DROPPED — MINOR
- Summary: "Federal workers who handle small-business loans already must stay out of loan decisions when they or close family have a financial conflict."
- Source (REPORT, minority views): "prohibits federal employees from participating in government matters in which they have a financial interest. This requirement extends to their spouse or minor children, their general partners, or any organization in which the employee serves as an officer, director, trustee, general partner, or employee."
- Source (CRS): "recuse themselves from such matter (with limited exceptions upon authorization from the employee's agency designee)".
- Why: "close family" is wider than "spouse or minor children" (and leaves out partners and organizations), and the "limited exceptions" are left off in this sentence. howItWorksToday items 2 and 3 state both correctly, so the page as a whole is not wrong. Rep. Williams (FLOOR) does speak of "loans to relatives or others who were barred from conflict of interest rules", which gives "close family" partial support as one member's statement.

### C4. headline, plainEnglish, changes row 1 ifPassed — CONDITION DROPPED — MINOR
- Summary (headline): "Requires small-business loan workers to sign conflict forms before handling loans." (plainEnglish): "a written form before each loan they help create, review, or approve". (changes row 1): "before personally helping create, review, or approve each agency loan".
- Source (CITED Sec. 2(a)): "an employee of the Administration that will personally and substantially participate in the origination, review, or approval of a loan".
- Why: "substantially" is dropped everywhere; plainEnglish and the headline drop "personally" too. The summary's group (anyone who helps) is wider than the bill's. The CRS summary also omits the qualifier ("employees who participate in the origination, review, or approval"), so the looser form has a source.
- Side note (not counted): "small-business loan workers" in the headline and in details.question could be read as including private lenders' staff; the bill covers only "an employee of the Administration". plainEnglish says "Federal workers", which cures it on the page.

### C5. howItWorksToday item 2 — EXACT WORD CHANGED — MINOR
- Summary: "unless an ethics official allows an exception."
- Source (CRS): "(with limited exceptions upon authorization from the employee's agency designee)".
- Why: no source calls the designee an "ethics official"; "limited" is dropped.

### C6. howItWorksToday item 3 — EXACT WORD CHANGED — MINOR
- Summary: "Current rules can cover the worker, a spouse, minor child, business partner, or an organization where the worker serves or works."
- Source (REPORT, minority views): "their spouse or minor children, their general partners, or any organization in which the employee serves as an officer, director, trustee, general partner, or employee."
- Why: "general partners" became "business partner" (a near term, not the exact one). The passage is from the Minority Views (Ranking Member Velazquez), shown as plain fact; it is a description of the statute, so not counted as a wrong credit.

### C7. howItWorksToday item 4 — OTHER (source cited does not say it in this form) — MINOR
- Summary: "A former loan officer approved pandemic loans for herself and relatives after the agency first denied them." (cited REPORT)
- Source (REPORT, minority views): "she submitted a fraudulent Economic Injury Disaster Loan (EIDL) application for $170,000. The SBA initially declined the application but in July 2021, Ms. Barrett approved the loan for herself, and other loans submitted by relatives".
- Source (FLOOR, Rep. Williams): "When the SBA initially denied the loans, the SBA employee approved them herself."
- Why: the cited report says only her own application was first declined; that the relatives' loans were also first denied rests on Rep. Williams's floor wording. "Former loan officer" is also Williams's/Olszewski's term (the report says "an employee of the Small Business Administration"). The sentence, prison term (14 months) and restitution ($350,849) match the report.
