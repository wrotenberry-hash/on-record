## Sec. 2 as passed by the House (EH; same in Cong. Rec. H5605)

> SEC. 2. FINDINGS. Congress finds the following:
> (1) Banking regulators continue to examine and monitor depository institutions without sufficient access to
> real-time information.
> (2) Risk surrounding technology procurement may present challenges for updating supervisory technology.
> (3) To ensure that prudential supervision is effective and sustainable in the digital age, agencies must leverage
> new technologies to allow for the financial monitoring necessary to preserve a safe and sound banking system.
> (4) New technological tools are also necessary in order for agencies to effectively fulfill mandates other than
> prudential supervision, including their mandates to assure consumer protection and monitor Bank Secrecy Act
> compliance.
> (5) Agencies' reliance on outdated technology can create vulnerabilities for the financial system, through--
> (A) difficulties in collecting, compiling, and analyzing relevant information about risks and noncompliance at
> supervised firms; (B) reliance on information that is inaccurate, incomplete, or not timely; (C) reliance on
> limited and outdated tools for data analysis; (D) difficulties in using data to identify risk trends;
> (E) difficulties in producing accurate and timely reports; (F) inadequacy of cybersecurity safeguards; and
> (G) failure to detect illegal activities.
> (6) The rapid expansion of financial firms' use of artificial intelligence may generate opportunities to improve
> the financial system while also introducing a range of risks, making it essential that agencies be equipped with
> the technology, expertise, and skills needed to analyze these opportunities and potential risks.
> (7) While agencies assess their supervisory capabilities on an ongoing basis, it is imperative that there be a
> unified goal of enhancing supervisory technologies that ensure effective and sustainable oversight.

## Statement 1 — VERIFIED (wording note)

- Summary (howItWorksToday #1, cited Sec. 2): "The bill says these agencies often lack enough real-time information."
- Official text, Sec. 2(1): "Banking regulators continue to examine and monitor depository institutions without
  sufficient access to real-time information."
- Also H. Rept. 119-711, Section-by-Section, Section 2: "Section 2 provides that Congress finds that financial
  regulators lack access to real-time information and should leverage modern technologies to improve supervision".
- Verdict: VERIFIED. The attribution ("The bill says") is right: this is a congressional finding in the bill.
  "lack enough real-time information" matches "without sufficient access to real-time information".
- Wording notes (minor, not a misstatement):
  - "often" is not in the bill. The finding is flat ("continue to examine and monitor ... without sufficient
    access"); the summary's "often" is its own softener and says slightly less than the bill, not more.
  - The finding speaks of "Banking regulators" and "depository institutions"; the summary's "these agencies" points
    back to agencies that examine "banks, credit unions and other financial firms". The committee report's own
    paraphrase widens it the same way ("financial regulators").

## Statement 2 — VERIFIED

- Summary (howItWorksToday #2, cited Sec. 2): "Agencies assess their supervision abilities on an ongoing basis."
  Also changes row 1 `today`: "Agencies assess their ability to supervise firms on an ongoing basis."
- Official text, Sec. 2(7): "While agencies assess their supervisory capabilities on an ongoing basis, it is
  imperative that there be a unified goal of enhancing supervisory technologies that ensure effective and
  sustainable oversight."
- Verdict: VERIFIED, close to word for word ("supervisory capabilities" -> "supervision abilities" / "ability to
  supervise firms"; "on an ongoing basis" kept exactly).
- Note: the bill states this as a congressional finding. howItWorksToday #2 cites Sec. 2, so the source is shown;
  changes row 1 `today` states it as plain fact with the row's source given as Sec. 3 rather than Sec. 2. The
  statement itself is supported either way.

## Statement 3 — VERIFIED (paraphrase note)

- Summary (howItWorksToday #1, cited Sec. 2): "Federal agencies examine banks, credit unions and other financial
  firms for safety, consumer protection and financial-crime rules."
- Official text:
  - "examine": Sec. 2(1) "Banking regulators continue to examine and monitor depository institutions".
  - "safety": Sec. 2(3) "prudential supervision ... the financial monitoring necessary to preserve a safe and sound
    banking system."
  - "consumer protection" and "financial-crime rules": Sec. 2(4) "mandates other than prudential supervision,
    including their mandates to assure consumer protection and monitor Bank Secrecy Act compliance."
  - "banks, credit unions and other financial firms": Sec. 2(1) "depository institutions"; Sec. 2(5)(A)
    "supervised firms"; Sec. 2(6) "financial firms"; Sec. 3(c) covered agencies include "the National Credit Union
    Administration" and "the Federal Housing Finance Agency"; Rep. Stutzman (Cong. Rec., Sept. 14, 2026) "Federal
    bank and credit union regulators".
- Verdict: VERIFIED. All three mandates are named in Sec. 2(3)-(4).
- Paraphrase note: "financial-crime rules" is the summary's plain-language stand-in for "Bank Secrecy Act
  compliance". The bill does not use the words "financial crime" in Sec. 2; within the official text the nearest
  support is Sec. 3(c) (covers "the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network") and Sec. 2(5)(G) ("failure to detect
  illegal activities"). Reasonable rendering, not an added fact.
