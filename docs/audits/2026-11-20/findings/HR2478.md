# Audit findings: H.R. 2478, Financial Exploitation Prevention Act of 2025

Packet: `packets/HR2478.txt` (51 lines, 36,976 chars, read in full). Only the packet was used.

**Verdict: MINOR ONLY.** No statement was found that tells the reader something false. Numbers, dates, vote counts and
names all check out against the packet (15 business days, 10 more business days, 2 days, 1 year, age 65 / age 18, $28
billion, $2 million over 2026-2031, 414-2 on June 25 2026, Senate passage without amendment on November 18 2026, Wagner
floor statement June 24 2026).

Counts: CORE 0 MISSTATES, 7 MINOR. EXTENDED 0 MISSTATES, 8 MINOR.

## CORE (headline, plainEnglish, howItWorksToday, changes)

### C1. Kind 5 ABOUT THE SOURCES — changes row "Contact person for direct mutual fund accounts", `today` — MINOR
- Summary: "The sources do not say current federal law requires these direct mutual fund accounts to request a trusted contact."
- Source: no passage; this is wording about the sources themselves. The packet contains no statement of current law on
  contact persons.
- Also in the same cell: "trusted contact" is a term no source uses. Sec. 2 says "the name and contact information of at
  least one individual who-- (i) is at the time of such request an adult; and (ii) may be contacted with respect to such
  account".
- Severity: MINOR (not false, but it is the reader-facing "the sources do not say" wording the brief lists).

### C2. Kind 3 CONDITION DROPPED — howItWorksToday item 4 — MINOR
- Summary: "The House committee says financial exploitation means theft of an older or disabled person's assets by deceit,
  threats, pressure, or false claims."
- Source (REPORT, Background): "Federal law defines this crime as the theft of an older or disabled person's assets by
  someone in a position of trust or by a targeted individual. The perpetrator often uses deceit, false pretenses,
  coercion, or threats to take the victim's money."
- Fault: two sentences merged. "often uses" becomes part of the definition ("means theft ... by deceit ..."), and the
  definition's own qualifier "by someone in a position of trust or by a targeted individual" is left off. The report also
  says "Federal law defines", which the summary gives as what "the House committee says" (acceptable, since the committee
  is the one reporting it, but the reader is not told it is presented as the federal-law definition).
- Severity: MINOR.

### C3. Kind 3 CONDITION DROPPED — changes row "Extra pause after review", `ifPassed` — MINOR
- Summary: "The pause may last 10 more business days if the fund or account-processing company still reasonably believes
  exploitation occurred, is occurring, or was attempted."
- Source (Sec. 2, new (i)(2)(B)): "may be extended by an additional 10 business days if the registered open-end
  investment company or a transfer agent ... (i) reasonably believes that ... ; (ii) ... not later than 2 days after
  making a determination under clause (i), notifies the individuals specified ...; (iii) initiates an internal review ...;
  (iv) holds amounts related to the delayed payment ... in a demand deposit account; and (v) documents and retains
  records ...".
- Fault: the extension is conditioned on five things joined by "and"; the row gives only the first as the "if". The
  notice and holding conditions do appear in later rows as "must" duties, and the internal review is not mentioned in the
  changes rows at all.
- Severity: MINOR.

### C4. OTHER (timing trigger changed) — changes row "Notice during an extended pause", `ifPassed` — MINOR
- Summary: "Within 2 days after extending the pause, the company must try to notify the customer's listed contact ..."
- Source (Sec. 2, (i)(2)(B)(ii)): "not later than 2 days after making a determination under clause (i), notifies the
  individuals specified by such security holder ... of the extension ... and the reason for such extension".
- Fault: the 2 days run from the determination (the reasonable belief), not from the extension. Rest of the row is
  supported: "must try" rests on (D)(ii) "makes a reasonable effort to contact"; the exception rests on (D)(i)
  "reasonably believes that an individual required to be notified ... is, has been, or will subject the security holder
  ... to financial exploitation".
- Severity: MINOR.

### C5. Kind 3 CONDITION DROPPED — plainEnglish and changes row 1 `ifPassed` (the opt-in) — MINOR
- Summary (plainEnglish): "This bill lets a fund pause payment for up to 15 business days, plus 10 more, if it suspects
  fraud ..." Summary (row 1): "The fund or its account-processing company may pause payment for up to 15 business days
  ..."
- Source (Sec. 2, new (h)(1)(B)): "Paragraph (2) and subsection (i) shall only apply to a registered open-end investment
  company and a transfer agent that have made the election under subparagraph (A)." (A): "may elect to comply ... by
  notifying the Commission of such election."
- Fault: the pause power exists only for a fund and transfer agent that have opted in by notifying the SEC. The CORE
  parts never say so (the word "Participating" appears only in the contact-person row; `whatItDoes` and `unknowns` do
  carry it). Still true that the bill "lets" any fund do this, so not false.
- Severity: MINOR.

### C6. OTHER / Kind 3 — howItWorksToday item 2 (scope of the bill) — MINOR
- Summary: "The bill applies to accounts held directly with a mutual fund and handled by a company that processes
  ownership and payment records for the fund."
- Source (Sec. 2, new (h)(2)): "In the case of a customer who is a holder of a non-institutional account held directly
  with a registered open-end investment company and serviced by a transfer agent (a `direct-at-fund account'), the
  company and transfer agent shall-- (A) request from such customer ...". New (i)(1), the pause, reads: "may postpone
  ... redemption of any redeemable security ... if such company or agent reasonably believes that-- (A) the redemption
  is requested by a security holder who is a specified adult".
- Faults: (a) "non-institutional" is dropped; (b) the bill text attaches the direct-at-fund limit to the contact-person
  duties in (h)(2); the pause in (i) is not worded as limited to direct-at-fund accounts (CRS: "a security issued by an
  open-end investment management company and serviced by a transfer agent"). The summary states the limit for the whole
  bill. The (h) heading ("Requirements With Respect to Non-institutional Direct At-fund Accounts") and the election
  clause make the summary's reading plausible, so this is looser wording rather than a false statement.
- The transfer-agent gloss is supported by CRS: "Transfer agents facilitate certain transactions for corporations and
  investment companies, including dividend distribution and change of securities ownership."
- Severity: MINOR.

### C7. Kind 4 NO SOURCE (inferred, not stated) — the 7-day current-law rule; plainEnglish, howItWorksToday item 1, changes row 1 `today` — MINOR
- Summary: "Today a mutual fund generally must pay within 7 days when a customer cashes out shares." / "current law
  generally requires payment within 7 days." / "A mutual fund generally must pay within 7 days after a customer asks to
  cash out shares."
- Source: no packet source states current law (there is no SOURCE USC section for 15 U.S.C. 80a-22(e)). The only footing
  is the bill's own wording (Sec. 2, new (i)(1)): "Notwithstanding subsection (e), ... may postpone the date of payment
  or satisfaction upon redemption ... for more than seven days after the tender of such security".
- Fault: the seven-day rule is a fair inference from that wording; "generally" (i.e. that current law has exceptions) is
  not stated anywhere in the packet.
- Severity: MINOR.

### CORE statements checked and found supported
- Headline "Lets mutual funds pause withdrawals when older adults may be scammed." — CRS: "establishes procedures for
  delaying the redemption of certain securities"; Waters (FLOOR): "allowing for mutual fund companies to delay the
  redemption or sale of mutual funds". Plain-language simplification; impaired adults are covered in the body.
- "up to 15 business days, plus 10 more" — Sec. 2 (i)(2)(A) "not more than 15 business days"; (B) "an additional 10
  business days".
- "if it suspects" / "reasonably suspects" — bill says "reasonably believes"; REPORT says "reasonably suspects" and the
  CBO text in FLOOR says "when they suspect". Accepted as sourced.
- "someone 65 or older, or an adult unable to protect their own interests" and howItWorksToday item 3 — Sec. 2 (i)(3).
- "The House passed it 414-2 on June 25, 2026, and the Senate passed it without changes on November 18, 2026." — STATUS:
  "Agreed to by the Yeas and Nays: (2/3 required): 414 - 2 (Roll no. 227)"; "Passed Senate without amendment by
  Unanimous Consent."
- "A mutual fund pools money from many people and invests it." — CRS: "Open-end investment management companies offer
  securities in pooled investment vehicles such as mutual funds." Plain gloss, accepted.
- howItWorksToday item 5 ($28 billion, AARP estimate) — REPORT: "with the American Association of Retired Persons
  estimating that financial exploitation costs seniors over $28 billion annually."
- Contact-person row `ifPassed` — Sec. 2 (h)(2)(A)-(B).
- "Where the delayed money is kept" row — the bill text makes the demand-deposit holding a condition of the 10-day
  extension only ((i)(2)(B)(iv)), but CRS ("In the event of delay, the company must hold the amounts related to the
  redemption in a demand deposit account") and REPORT ("During this period, the funds must be held in a demand deposit
  account") state it the way the summary does. "bank account where funds can be withdrawn on demand" is a plain gloss of
  "demand deposit account"; the packet does not define the term. "record it on the next account statement" — (B)(v)
  "includes such records in the first required account statement of the security holder provided after the date on which
  the determination is made". Not counted as a fault.
- "Federal study" row — Sec. 2(b)(1): "Not later than 1 year after the date of the enactment ... shall submit to
  Congress a report that includes recommendations regarding the regulatory and legislative changes necessary".
- `today` cells "Current law does not create this extra pause ...", "does not include the bill's notice rule ...", etc.
  — not stated by any source; they follow from the bill adding new subsections (h) and (i) "at the end" of section 22.
  Treated as fair inference, not counted.
- Note (not a listed fault kind): howItWorksToday items 2 and 3 describe what the bill covers, not how things work
  today.
