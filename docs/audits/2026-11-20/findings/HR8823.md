# Audit findings: H.R. 8823 (Putting Patients First by Strengthening Provider Accountability in FECA Act)

Packet: `packets/HR8823.txt` (read in full). Sources present: CITED Sec. 2, CONTEXT, HEADINGS, SOURCE CRS, SOURCE FLOOR,
SOURCE STATUS, SOURCE USC 18 §24, SOURCE USC 5 §8103. No SOURCE REPORT, SOURCE CBO or SOURCE RULE in the packet.

**VERDICT: MISSTATES** (CORE: 2 MISSTATES, 5 MINOR. EXTENDED: 0 MISSTATES, 8 MINOR.)

---

## CORE

### C1. plainEnglish | WRONG CREDIT | MISSTATES
- Summary: "Current rules can exclude providers convicted of fraud, but **speakers said** those rules may not stop payment on
  claims already submitted before conviction."
- Source (FLOOR, Rep. Takano only): "Those rules do not enable the Department to stop payment on any claims that a convicted
  provider has already submitted prior to the conviction."
- The other two speakers said something materially different:
  - FLOOR, Rep. Mackenzie: "Currently, DOL has the authority to suspend payments to medical providers who are convicted of
    fraudulent activity. This authority has not been codified and could be rescinded at any time."
  - FLOOR, Rep. Walberg: "It codifies existing Department of Labor policy to prevent medical providers who are convicted of
    defrauding FECA from continuing to receive taxpayer-funded payments through the program."
- Fault: one member's claim is credited to "speakers" (plural). Only Takano said it; the sponsor and the floor manager
  described the bill as codifying authority Labor already has. (The `changes` row 2 "today" cell credits Takano correctly.)
- Lesser point in the same sentence: "may not stop" softens Takano's "do not enable".

### C2. changes row 2 ("Claims submitted before conviction"), ifPassed | CONDITION DROPPED / EXACT WORD CHANGED | MISSTATES
- Summary: "Labor may suspend payments to that provider for covered services, medical devices, supplies, or agency-paid
  initial expenses, **including** payments made 180 days after enactment."
- Source (Sec. 2(b)): "The amendments made by this Act shall apply with respect to payments made to a provider of services,
  appliances, or supplies **on or after the date that is 180 days after** the date of enactment of this Act."
- Fault: the source's limit (only payments made on or after day 180) is turned into an example ("including"), which tells the
  reader the authority also reaches payments made before that date. Rows 3 and plainEnglish state the timing as a limit, so
  the summary is also internally inconsistent.

### C3. headline (and plainEnglish "stop those payments") | EXACT WORD CHANGED + CONDITION DROPPED | MINOR
- Summary: "Lets Labor **stop paying** medical providers convicted of fraud."
- Source (Sec. 2): "The Secretary of Labor may **suspend** payments to a provider ... if the provider has been convicted of
  fraud **with respect to** (A) this subchapter; (B) any Federal health care benefit program ...; or (C) any State program
  ..." CRS: "authorizes the Department of Labor to suspend payments ... to **certain** providers convicted of fraud."
- Fault: "suspend" becomes "stop paying", and the limit to fraud tied to the listed programs is left off. Rated MINOR because
  the bill's own long title as read on the floor says "to allow the Secretary of Labor to suspend payments to medical
  providers who have been convicted of fraud" (FLOOR, Walberg motion), and Takano himself uses "stop payment".

### C4. plainEnglish | WRONG CREDIT (stated as plain fact) | MINOR
- Summary: "This bill lets the Department of Labor stop **those payments** 180 days after it becomes law" ("those payments" =
  payments on claims submitted before conviction).
- Source (Sec. 2): "may suspend payments to a provider ... if the provider has been convicted of fraud" - the bill text does
  not mention claims submitted before conviction. That the bill reaches those claims is Takano's characterisation (FLOOR:
  "this bill closes an important gap").
- Also: "stop those payments 180 days after it becomes law" vs Sec. 2(b) "payments made ... on or after the date that is 180
  days after the date of enactment" (reads as a single date rather than a start date).

### C5. changes row 3 ("When the new power applies"), ifPassed | CONDITION DROPPED | MINOR
- Summary: "applies to payments made 180 days after the bill becomes law."
- Source (Sec. 2(b)): "payments made ... **on or after** the date that is 180 days after the date of enactment".
- Fault: "on or after" dropped; literally read, it covers only payments made on day 180.

### C6. changes rows 3 and 4 (label "When the new power applies"; "new suspension authority"; "new payment-suspension authority") | WRONG CREDIT (stated as plain fact) | MINOR
- Summary presents the authority as new, as plain fact.
- Source: only Takano calls it new (FLOOR: "rules for implementing the new authority"). FLOOR, Mackenzie: "Currently, DOL has
  the authority to suspend payments ... This authority has not been codified"; "codify the Department of Labor's ability".
  FLOOR, Walberg: "codifies existing Department of Labor policy". CRS: "explicitly authorizes".
- Fault: a contested characterisation (new power vs codifying an existing one) stated as fact. As a statutory matter the
  subsection is new, hence MINOR.

### C7. howItWorksToday item 3 | EXACT WORD CHANGED + CONDITION DROPPED | MINOR
- Summary: "The Department of Labor **can approve payment** for care that is likely to cure, relieve, or reduce the worker's
  disability."
- Source (USC 5 §8103(a)): "The United States **shall furnish** ... the services, appliances, and supplies **prescribed or
  recommended by a qualified physician**, which the Secretary of Labor considers likely to cure, give relief, reduce the
  degree or the period of disability, **or aid in lessening the amount of the monthly compensation**."
- Fault: "shall furnish" becomes "can approve payment"; the qualified-physician condition and the fourth ground are dropped.
  (The related "when authorized or approved by the Secretary" condition on Fund payment is also absent from item 1.)

### Checked in CORE and not faulted
- howItWorksToday 1, 2, 4, 5: supported by USC 5 §8103(a) and CRS; item 5 correctly credited to Rep. Mackenzie.
- changes row 1 (today / ifPassed): matches CRS and Sec. 2(c)(1)(A)-(C).
- changes row 2 "today": correctly credited to Rep. Takano.
- changes row 3 "today" ("No new statutory (law) start date applies because the bill is not law"): no source states it, but
  it is a truism consistent with STATUS; not counted.
- changes row 4: "must write regulations" = Sec. 2 "shall promulgate regulations".
- plainEnglish vote/date/status: STATUS "396 - 0 (Roll no. 251)", July 20, 2026; "Received in the Senate" July 21, 2026.

### E5. whatItDoes item 2 | EXACT WORD CHANGED | MINOR
- Summary: "certain initial expenses **paid by** the injured worker's employing agency".
- Source (Sec. 2): "vouchers or certifications described in subsection (b) for the expenses **incurred by** the employing
  agency with respect to such a provider"; USC 5 §8103(b): "The Secretary may certify vouchers for these expenses **out of

- Fault: "incurred by" becomes "paid by"; under §8103(b) the vouchers are paid from the Fund.

- Source: USC 5 §8103 does not mention taxpayers. The taxpayer effect is members' argument: FLOOR, Walberg "protects both
  taxpayers and the Federal employees"; FLOOR, Takano "protects taxpayers".
