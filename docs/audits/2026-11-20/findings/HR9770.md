## CORE findings

### C1 — plainEnglish (and headline, details.question) — CONDITION DROPPED — MINOR
- Summary: "This bill keeps most agencies running through December 4, 2026" / headline "Funds federal agencies through
  December 4 to avoid a shutdown."
- Source (CRS): "provides continuing FY2027 appropriations to federal agencies through the earlier of December 4, 2026, or
  the enactment of the applicable appropriations act." Full text Sec. 106: "available until whichever of the following
  first occurs".
- The "whichever comes first" condition is left off here. It is given correctly in `changes` row 1 and whatItDoes 1, and
  both floor speakers also say "until December 4", so same practical meaning.

### C2 — plainEnglish — WRONG CREDIT (one member's claim stated as plain fact) — MINOR
- Summary: "The House passed it 220–205 on July 21, 2026, amid a fight over immigration-enforcement limits."
- Source: the vote and date are in STATUS ("Passed by the Yeas and Nays: 220 - 205 (Roll no. 272)"). The "fight" rests
  only on Rep. DeLauro's floor statement (FLOOR): "without implementing any of the protections that Democrats have sought
  ... a clear line in the sand that Democrats have drawn for months." Rep. Cole (FLOOR) says the bill "contains no poison
  pills or partisan riders." No source states as fact that the vote turned on this, and no source uses
  "immigration-enforcement limits"; DeLauro speaks of Border Patrol funding and "protections".

### C3 — howItWorksToday item 3 — EXACT WORD CHANGED — MINOR
- Summary: "Congress often passes a stopgap law."
- Source (PRIMER): "Congress usually passes a stopgap law (called a continuing resolution)."
- "usually" became "often" (weaker, not false).

### C4 — howItWorksToday item 5 — NO SOURCE (gloss) — MINOR
- Summary: "Current law sets timing rules for dividing approved money among agencies."
- Source (USC 31 §1513): deadlines by which an appropriation "shall be apportioned" and by which "The President shall
  apportion in writing an appropriation available to an executive agency". Full text Sec. 108: "the time limitations for
  submission and approval of apportionments".
- The packet never says apportionment divides money "among agencies"; it is done to an appropriation already available to
  an agency (subsection (c) speaks of dividing "among the major organizational units of the department"). Loose
  description; the timing-rule point itself is supported.

### C5 — howItWorksToday item 5 — EXACT WORD CHANGED (broadened) — MINOR
- Summary: "...but keeps other spending-control rules."
- Source (Full text Sec. 108): "nothing in this Act may be construed to waive any other provision of law governing the
  apportionment of funds."
- The source keeps other apportionment law specifically, not "spending-control rules" generally.

### C6 — changes row 1 ("How long agencies can keep spending"), ifPassed — NO SOURCE (added word) — MINOR
- Summary: "through the earliest of a new full-year spending law, a spending law that omits that work, or December 4, 2026."
- Source (Full text Sec. 106): "(1) The enactment into law of an appropriation for any project or activity provided for in
  this Act. (2) The enactment into law of the applicable appropriations Act for fiscal year 2027 without any provision for
  such project or activity. (3) December 4, 2026."
- Trigger (1) is any enacted appropriation for the project or activity; "full-year" is not in the text.

### C7 — changes row 3 ("Starting new projects"), today — NO SOURCE — MINOR
- Summary: "New yearly spending laws could fund new projects if Congress passes them."
- Source: nothing in the packet says this. PRIMER says only "each year Congress must pass new spending bills." An
  inference, not a sourced statement.

## EXTENDED findings

### E1 — whatItDoes item 2 — CONDITION DROPPED — MINOR (the most serious extended item)
- Summary: "Bars most new projects and Defense Department new production, higher production rates, or new multi-year
  buying starts during the stopgap period."
- Source (Full text Sec. 102(b)): no Defense funds "shall be used to initiate multi-year procurements utilizing advance
  procurement funding for economic order quantity procurement unless specifically appropriated later."
- The bar covers only multi-year procurements using advance procurement funding for economic order quantity, and has an
  exception ("unless specifically appropriated later"). The summary states it for all new multi-year buying.

### E2 — caseFor item 2 (Rep. Womack) — CONDITION DROPPED — MINOR
- Summary: "citing a past shutdown that affected 6 million people and cost travel businesses $150 million a day."
- Source (FLOOR, Womack): "The results were canceled or delayed flights and ground stops at airports across the Nation. By
  the end of the shutdown, Mr. Speaker, 6 million people had been affected, not to mention the estimated costs incurred by
  the U.S. travel sector of $150 million a day."
- "estimated" is dropped from the cost figure, and the 6 million is given in the context of flight disruption, not the
  shutdown overall. Credit to Womack is correct.

### E3 — caseAgainst item 2 (Rep. DeLauro) — EXACT WORD CHANGED — MINOR
- Summary: "She says it omits limits on money transfers to Border Patrol and lacks protections such as body cameras,
  warrant requirements, and limits at schools, hospitals, and houses of worship."
- Source (FLOOR, DeLauro): the April bill "included a provision to remove all of the funding for the Border Patrol ...
  That prohibition is removed by this continuing resolution, which would allow Homeland Security Secretary Mullin to
  transfer billions of dollars to the Border Patrol"; protections "like mandating the use of body-worn cameras ...
  prohibiting operations at sensitive locations like schools, hospitals, and houses of worship; requiring agents to get a
  warrant from a judge".
- "prohibition" / "prohibiting operations" softened to "limits" twice. Credit is correct.

### E4 — unknowns item 2 — ABOUT THE SOURCES — MINOR
