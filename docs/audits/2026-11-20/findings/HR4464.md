## CORE findings

### C1. headline — MISSTATES
- Kind: 2 EXACT WORD CHANGED (allows/requires) and 3 CONDITION DROPPED
- Summary: "Requires longer savings estimates for prevention-focused health bills."
- Source (CITED Sec. 2): "Upon a request by the chairman and ranking minority member of the Committee on the Budget ... and chairman and ranking minority member of the committee of primary jurisdiction ... the Director shall determine if proposed legislation would result in net reductions in budget outlays in budgetary outyears through the use of preventive health care." and "If the Director determines that proposed legislation would result in net reductions ... the Director-- (A) shall include ... a description and estimate of the reductions ... and (B) may prepare a budget projection that includes some or all of the budgetary outyears".
- Source (CRS): "requires the Congressional Budget Office (CBO), upon receiving a request from Congress, to determine if ..."
- Source (FLOOR, Obernolte): "by allowing the Congressional Budget Office, upon bipartisan request by a committee of jurisdiction, to instead evaluate potential Federal savings". (FLOOR, DeGette): "by allowing committees to request an extended estimate". (FLOOR, Boyle): "this allows, on a bipartisan basis, a request for a 30-year analysis".
- Why: nothing is required for prevention-focused bills as a class. A longer look happens only when the four named chairs/ranking members ask, and a savings estimate follows only if the Director finds net reductions. The summary's own plainEnglish says "lets certain congressional leaders ask", which contradicts the headline.

### C2. plainEnglish — MINOR
- Kind: 2 EXACT WORD CHANGED (near word)
- Summary: "this bill lets certain congressional leaders ask for extra estimates"
- Source (CITED Sec. 2): "the chairman and ranking minority member of the Committee on the Budget ... and the chairman and ranking minority member of the committee of primary jurisdiction"
- Why: these are committee chairs and ranking members, not "congressional leaders" in the usual sense. whatItDoes point 1 has it right ("paired majority and minority committee leaders"). Same meaning for a careful reader; looser word.

### C3. howItWorksToday item 5 — MINOR
- Kind: 3 CONDITION DROPPED / scope of a qualifier changed
- Summary: "The bill defines preventive health care as action to protect health and prevent disease, disability and premature death, backed by credible public evidence."
- Source (CITED Sec. 2): "an action that focuses on the health of the public, individuals, and defined populations in order to protect, promote, and maintain health and wellness and prevent disease, disability, and premature death, including through the promotion and use of effective, innovative health care interventions that are demonstrated by credible and publicly available evidence from epidemiological projection models, clinical trials ..."
- Why: in the bill the evidence wording qualifies the "including through ... interventions" clause; the summary makes it a requirement of the whole definition. (Also: this item is the bill's definition, placed under "how it works today"; it is labelled "The bill defines", so not counted as a separate fault.)

### C4. changes row 2 "How far the estimate looks", ifPassed — MINOR
- Kind: 2 EXACT WORD CHANGED (shall/may)
- Summary: "For qualifying preventive health bills, the budget office may estimate savings in two extra 10-year periods after the first 10 years."
- Source (CITED Sec. 2): "(A) shall include, in any projection prepared by the Director on such proposed legislation, a description and estimate of the reductions in budget outlays in the budgetary outyears ...; and (B) may prepare a budget projection that includes some or all of the budgetary outyears"
- Why: the savings estimate is "shall" once the Director finds reductions; the "may" belongs to a wider budget projection covering the outyears. Row 3 states the "must describe and estimate" duty correctly, so the reader gets the right picture across the two rows; hence MINOR.

### C5. changes row 2, today — MINOR
- Kind: 4 NO SOURCE (gloss)
- Summary: "direct spending (spending that happens automatically under existing law)"
- Source: SOURCE REPORT uses "direct spending" without defining it ("cost estimates of changes in direct spending and revenues generally cover the current year, the budget year, and the subsequent nine years"). No packet source defines the term.
- Why: the definition in brackets is supplied from outside the packet.

### C6. changes row 4 "Whether later savings can satisfy budget rules", today — MINOR
- Kind: 4 NO SOURCE
- Summary: "Standard cost estimates are used to judge bills under congressional budget rules."
- Source: nearest passages are SOURCE REPORT "Cost estimates prepared by CBO ... serve as a guide of the fiscal impact of legislation considered by Congress" and FLOOR (Obernolte) "This bill does not change existing budget enforcement rules ... it does not allow projected future savings to be used to offset spending today."
- Why: no packet source says in terms that standard estimates are used to determine compliance with budget rules; it is implied by the bill's limitation clause, not stated.

### CORE statements checked and found supported
- plainEnglish "usually estimates a bill's budget effects over 10 years": REPORT "CBO's current scoring window for cost estimates is generally 10 years".
- plainEnglish "extra estimates covering the next 20 years": Sec. 2 "the 2 consecutive 10-year periods beginning with the first fiscal year that is 10 years after the current fiscal year".
- plainEnglish "could not be used to meet budget rules": Sec. 2 "may not be used to determine compliance with the Congressional Budget Act of 1974 or any other budgetary enforcement controls".
- plainEnglish "The House passed it 401–0 on November 19, 2026": STATUS "Agreed to by the Yeas and Nays: (2/3 required): 401 - 0 (Roll no. 327)", November 19, 2026.
- plainEnglish "after supporters said 10 years can miss later health savings": Obernolte ("often fails to capture potential savings"), DeGette ("savings don't always accrue within the 10 years"), Boyle ("entirely missed in a CBO projection"). Plural is right.
- plainEnglish "savings from early treatment, screenings or vaccines": examples, not from the bill. Early treatment is Obernolte ("identifying and treating diseases earlier"); vaccines is Arrington ("lifesaving therapeutics, vaccines, or better prevention"); "screenings" matches REPORT "detecting it early" in sense only. Not counted.
- howItWorksToday 1: REPORT "providing objective, nonpartisan information to lawmakers through cost estimates of legislation".
- howItWorksToday 2: USC 2 §602(a), (b), (c)(1), (c)(3).
- howItWorksToday 3: REPORT, exact.
- howItWorksToday 4: REPORT "preventive health care can generate budgetary savings when longer-term effects are examined, but ... may not be apparent when only analyzing the first 10 years"; "lessening the incidence of a disease or by detecting it early".
- changes row 1 today / ifPassed: USC 2 §602(c); Sec. 2 para (1). "in either chamber ... together" is right.
- changes row 3 ifPassed: Sec. 2 para (2)(A).
- changes row 4 ifPassed: Sec. 2 para (3).
