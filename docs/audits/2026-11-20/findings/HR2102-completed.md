Who is speaking: every passage quoted below is from the Congressional Budget Office cost estimate, "H.R. 2102, MAJOR
RICHARD STAR ACT, AS MODIFIED BY H. RES. 1247", printed in the Record at Rep. Bost's request ("Mr. Speaker, I include in

**CORE RESULT: ALL VERIFIED** (six statements; three small notes, none a misstatement).

## 1. "about 58,000" people affected — VERIFIED

- Summary (headline): "Lets about 58,000 combat-injured military retirees keep full retirement pay."
- Summary (plainEnglish): "adding about $1,450 a month on average for about 58,000 people."
- Summary (howItWorksToday 5): "In September 2025, the Defense Department made those separate combat payments to about
  58,000 medically retired people who served fewer than 20 years."
- Official text (CBO estimate, "Basis of estimate: Direct spending", page H6198, lines 926-929): "According to the DoD
  Office of the Actuary, in September 2025 the department made CRSC payments to about 58,000 Chapter 61 retirees who
  served fewer than 20 years. CBO expects that number to increase to about 65,000 by 2036."
- "Chapter 61 retirees" is defined in the same estimate (page H6197, lines 840-842): "service members who have retired
  from the armed forces because of a disability", so "medically retired" is a fair rendering. "CRSC" is
  "Combat-Related Special Compensation" (page H6198, line 907), the summary's "separate combat payment".
- Note (not a fault): 58,000 is a count of people paid in September 2025, reported by CBO from the Defense Department's
  actuary. The headline and plainEnglish use it as the number the bill would help; CBO itself expects the number to grow.
  howItWorksToday 5 states it with its date, exactly as the source does.
- Members' own figures in the debate differ slightly and are not the source: Houlahan "more than 50,000", Goodlander
  "nearly 60,000", Ciscomani "59,000", Conaway "over 50,000", Menefee "over 50,000".

## 2. "September 2025" — VERIFIED

- Summary (howItWorksToday 5): "In September 2025, the Defense Department made those separate combat payments ..."
- Official text (page H6198, lines 926-928): "in September 2025 the department made CRSC payments to about 58,000
  Chapter 61 retirees who served fewer than 20 years."
- "the department" in that sentence is the Department of Defense ("According to the DoD Office of the Actuary").

## 3. "$2,275" given up and "$825" got back a month, on average — VERIFIED

- Summary (changes row 1, today): "They give up about $2,275 a month of retirement pay and get about $825 back through
  the separate combat payment, on average."
- Summary (whoItAffects 2): "Affected retirees gave up about $2,275 a month and got about $825 back, on average."
- Official text (page H6198, lines 930-932): "The average disability offset for those retirees was about $2,275 per
  month, whereas the average CRSC payment was about $825 per month."
- "disability offset" is explained in the same estimate (page H6198, lines 897-899): "Military retirees who are eligible
  for disability compensation typically have their retired pay offset dollar-for-dollar by the amount of their
  disability compensation." So "give up ... of retirement pay" is right. Both numbers, "about", "per month" and
  "average" all match. "those retirees" are the 58,000 of September 2025, so the averages are for that group at that
  time.

## 4. "65,000 by 2036" — VERIFIED

- Summary (whoItAffects 1): "The budget office expects about 65,000 by 2036."
- Official text (page H6198, lines 928-929): "CBO expects that number to increase to about 65,000 by 2036."
- The credit to the budget office is right; "expects" is CBO's own verb.

## 5. "$1,450 on average" — VERIFIED; the Congressional Budget Office states it, not only Rep. Elfreth

- Summary (plainEnglish): "adding about $1,450 a month on average"; (changes row 1, ifPassed): "The average gain would
  be about $1,450 a month."; (whoItAffects 2): "The bill would add about $1,450 a month."
- Official text (CBO estimate, page H6198, lines 932-936): "Under H.R. 2102, those disability retirees would instead
  receive the full amount of their retired pay. Thus, they would receive about $1,450 more each month in total
  compensation under the bill than they will under current law."
- Who states it: the CBO estimate states it, as the difference between the two averages in the sentence before it
  ($2,275 − $825 = $1,450). Rep. Elfreth repeats it on the floor (page H6196, line 687): "On average, this would mean
  $1,450 more per month for those veterans." So giving it as fact with a FLOOR credit is sound; it is not one member's
  claim. The first findings' worry about a WRONG CREDIT does not apply.
- Note: CBO adds (lines 936-938) "(Both retired pay and disability compensation are adjusted upward based on inflation,
  so CBO projects those monthly amounts would increase over time.)" The summary does not say this; it does not need to.

## 6. Cost figures "$13 billion" and "$7 billion" — VERIFIED (both are CBO's own rounded words; period and kind match)

- Summary (changes row 5, ifPassed): "Direct federal spending rises by $13 billion from 2027 through 2036. Spending that
  depends on future funding decisions rises by $7 billion from 2027 through 2036."
- Summary (plainEnglish): "its $13 billion added federal spending from 2027 through 2036".

### $13 billion — direct spending, fiscal years 2027-2036
- Official text (CBO, page H6197, lines 886-889): "Direct spending: H.R. 2102 would increase compensation for certain
  disabled military retirees. CBO estimates that enacting the bill would increase direct spending by $13 billion over
  the 2027-2036 period (see Table 1)."
- Also (page H6198, lines 938-941): "CBO estimates that enacting the changes to CRSC would increase direct spending by
  almost $13 billion over the 2027-2036 period."
- Table (summary table, page H6197, line 810; "By fiscal year, millions of dollars"): "Direct Spending (Outlays) ... 800
  5,750 12,750" under columns "2027 2027-2031 2027-2036". Table 1 (lines 870-871): "INCREASES IN DIRECT SPENDING —
  Estimated Budget Authority ... 12,900; Estimated Outlays ... 12,750".
- So: $13 billion is CBO's own rounding of 12,750 million in outlays; the kind is direct spending (spending that happens
  without a yearly funding vote); the period is fiscal years 2027-2036. The same amount is the estimated increase in the
  deficit (line 812: "Increase or Decrease (-) in the Deficit 800 5,750 12,750").

### $7 billion — spending subject to appropriation, fiscal years 2027-2036
- Official text (CBO, page H6198, lines 957-960): "On that basis, CBO estimates that implementing H.R. 2102 would
  increase spending subject to appropriation by $7 billion over the 2027-2036 period. Any related spending would be
  subject to the appropriation of the estimated amounts."
- Table (page H6197, lines 813-814): "Spending Subject to Appropriation (Outlays) ... 0 3,100 7,450". Table 1 (lines
  873-876): "INCREASES IN SPENDING SUBJECT TO APPROPRIATION a — Estimated Authorization ... 7,450; Estimated Outlays ...
  7,450".
- So: $7 billion is CBO's own rounding of 7,450 million; the kind is spending subject to appropriation (the summary's
  "spending that depends on future funding decisions" is a fair plain rendering); the period is 2027-2036, though the
  table shows 0 for 2027 and CBO says the payments "would increase beginning in 2028" (line 885).
- What the $7 billion is: larger yearly payments by the Defense Department and the Coast Guard into the Military
  Retirement Fund (page H6198, lines 942-956: "DoD and the USCG make annual accrual payments to the Military Retirement
  Fund ... CBO expects that under the bill, each of those payments would grow by 3.6 percent beginning in 2028").

### Notes on statement 6 (none changes the verdict)
- Dropped footnote. Table 1 footnote "a" (line 878) on the $7.45 billion line reads: "Outlays for accrual payments to
  the Military Retirement Fund are intragovernmental transactions that have no net effect on federal spending." The
  summary puts the $7 billion under the label "Cost to the federal government" with no such note. The summary's
  sentence itself matches CBO's sentence, so this is a dropped qualifier, not a wrong number. Worth a human look: a
  reader will add $13 billion and $7 billion into a $20 billion cost (as Rep. Bost did: "a combined $20 billion in total
  spending", page H6197, line 799), while CBO's own footnote says the second amount has no net effect on federal
  spending and its deficit line shows only 12,750.
- "Estimate" dropped. The summary says spending "rises by"; CBO says "CBO estimates". The first findings already logged
  this as MINOR (C1). The other half of C1, that "$13 billion" and "$7 billion" lack "about", is answered: CBO's own prose
  says "$13 billion" and "$7 billion" flat (and once "almost $13 billion").
- plainEnglish credit. "some supporters said its $13 billion added federal spending ... should be paid for." No member
  spoke the figure $13 billion. Rep. Bost said "a combined $20 billion in total spending completely unpaid for" (H6197)
  and "we have to pay for these VA benefit expansions" (H6198, line 1043); Rep. Rogers of Alabama said "I just believe we
  should pay for it" (H6195, line 581). Read as the summary describing the cost in CBO's figure and supporters saying it
  should be paid for, the sentence holds. The first findings logged this as MINOR (C2); that stands.
